As stated above, my background is varied; but to that I would also add, not overtly maths orientated. My A-Levels are in English Language, Sociology and History, with an AS in Computing. My bachelors degree is in History, and after a few years in retail post graduation, I attended college to retrain in carpentry. My interest in computing has seen me work through some online programming courses, but nothing formal. I achieved a B in GCSE maths (I think), so nothing too showy. My main recollection of GCSE maths is a lot of my teacher doing maths on the board and me not having a clue what most of it meant.

I mention this because I think a lot of people get caught up in "how qualified" educators are in regard to the subjects they teach, even when the level of the content isn't anything an educated adult couldn't quickly pick up. I don't consider myself a specialist, but I am a strong generalist with the view that you can teach anyone anything, as long as you have the patience to find the right explanation. This has always helped me to bridge gaps in understanding in the various training roles in which I have served, helping and supporting colleagues and students on their learning journeys, regardless of the context and content.

As a generalist, I rely heavily on the work and resources provided by others in order to bring myself up to speed on whatever it is my learners need to learn. As much as I'd love to be able to compose my own personal treasure trove of high quality resources, I think that would be a poor prioritisation of my time in this moment. I think the most valuable thing I can do right now is collect and curate. My goal with this wiki therefore, is to find all the exciting, excellent, existing work that's out there, and assemble this wiki to point you towards it.

So that's who I am and why I'm doing what I'm doing. I hope you find this a valuable resource. If you do, please consider supporting the work of the many fantastic people and organisations this wiki signposts you to.
