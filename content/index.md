Welcome to Cal's Pedagogical Compendium. Here I hope you will find a variety of useful resources and information that will help you build your knowledge as an educator. This is a resource that I have started assembling in my first few years working in Further Education in the UK, so I am by no means an expert on these matters. However, whilst my own views, opinions and understanding are yet to be further tempered by further years of service and professional development, I hope that this site will serve as a hub from which you can venture out to find somebody who actually does know what they're talking about.

## What's different about this site?

Probably not much. However, I will ~~quickly~~ offer my "philosophy" on what I think I'm doing in case there's something in there.

There is a lot of great information out there, but it's not always easy to find, and the times we live in don't seem to be making it any easier. If anything, it feels harder and harder to find useful and reliable information.

In such times, the value of information is not just in its quality, and certainly not in the quantity. With the proliferation of near infinite low quality information produced by combinations of low effort, a lack of competency, and even with malicious intent, useful and reliable information is drowned out by a cacophony of ignorant and poorly considered views and opinions.

It is under these circumstances in which the value of quality information is increasingly defined by its organisation and accessibility. 

The goal of this site is to collect and organise this information, and put it somewhere you can easily find it, without having to trawl through everything else.

### A ramble about search engines, "Big Tech," and advertising

Search engines are increasingly unable to guide us towards the information we need; perhaps even unwilling when we consider that they have more to gain from our questions going unanswered. Each further search generates more advertising revenue for the small number of companies that dominate the  technology sector.

Even those that I link to from this site are vulnerable to influence by these conflicting interests. Their work requires a huge investment of time and resources just to produce that initial output. They then have to cut though all the noise or go unheard. Big tech and their distribution platforms funded by advertising revenue, are often the only option for those looking to establish themselves, meaning their independence is immediately compromised as they rely on these platforms and the attached advertisers as their only viable means of distribution.

There are a huge number advertisers with "solutions" in search of a problem, and they will offer good money to effective communicators who have established trust with their audiences just to have their products mentioned, or maybe even presented as a part of the solution. For this reason I urge you to support their work with whatever resources you can afford, in order to help them maintain their independence.

### Some not at all sourced historical ~~analysis~~ speculation

Technological shifts that enabled more people to more easily communicate information and knowledge have always been a net benefit to society. The invention of the printing press allowed knowledge to be reproduced and distributed at significantly lower cost than before. Information could now be reliably recorded  **rapidly reproduced** and **widely disseminated** as quickly as it could be carried, at a cost that meant the physical medium by which it was transmitted was now effectively disposable. Even at a time when literacy was very low, the way was opened for information to transfer efficiently from city to city, from town to town, from one community to thousands of others.

Most visiting will likely be unaware of the [Tolpuddle Martyrs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolpuddle_Martyrs), a group of six agricultural labourers from a small village in Dorset, that centuries later is inhabited by fewer than five hundred.
